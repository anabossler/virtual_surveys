\begin{abstract}
New regulations can require changes in products before firms and regulators
know how consumers will respond. Building on Bas's typology of approaches to
the future and its extension to synthetic foresight, we examine how large
language models (LLMs) can support the exploration of technology adoption
under these conditions. We present Bayesian Virtual Surveys (BVS), an
interactive foresight method combining national survey data, published
evidence and Bayesian simulation. LLMs assist evidence extraction and
translate users' questions into structured queries. A Bayesian network
represents relationships between consumer characteristics, while a transfer
model estimates responses not measured in the national survey. Human
researchers review the evidence and its mapping, and users remain responsible
for interpreting scenarios and deciding how to act. We apply BVS to
recycled-content food-contact packaging in Spain under
EU~Regulation~2025/40. The application explores how differences between
consumer groups may create different adoption risks under the same
regulatory requirement. The scenarios can inform the selection of pilot
groups, priorities for data collection and questions for public dialogue.
The methodological contribution is to connect LLM-assisted evidence
processing with probabilistic simulation and human review in a process for
exploring possible consumer responses. This approach extends the use of
synthetic foresight to decisions made before direct evidence on acceptance
is available. It also distinguishes the reproduction of observed survey
patterns from the validity of projected responses. These projections depend
on assumptions about transferring evidence between populations and have not
been validated in Spain.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} large language models; human--AI division of labour;
