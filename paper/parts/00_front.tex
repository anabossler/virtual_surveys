\begin{itemize}
\item Language models extract evidence and parse queries; a Bayesian engine computes.
\item Human audit corrected or excluded %%AUDCHANGED%% of %%AUDPROPOSED%% model-proposed evidence entries.

\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly proposed as research instruments
in technology management, but their outputs are fluent rather than verified.
We present Bayesian Virtual Surveys (BVS), a decision-support system in which
the division of labour between LLMs, a probabilistic engine and human
researchers is explicit and auditable, and apply it where a regulation fixes a
technology before the behavioural evidence needed to implement it exists. LLMs
extract statistics from published studies and translate natural-language
queries into structured profiles; they generate no probabilities. A Bayesian
network learned from national survey microdata generates the observed
population, and a documented transfer model attaches the outcome the survey
does not measure. Researchers review every extracted entry and own the transfer
assumptions. Applied to acceptance of recycled-content food-contact packaging
under EU~Regulation~2025/40, with Spanish microdata (CIS~3391,
$n = 2{,}254$) and 11 external studies, the human audit accepted
%%AUDACCEPTED%% of the %%AUDPROPOSED%% entries the LLM proposed for the target
outcomes as extracted, corrected %%AUDCORRECTED%% and excluded
%%AUDEXCLUDED%%; construct mismatch was the most frequent error, and
LLM-mapped evidence on observed variables degraded fidelity. The synthetic
population reproduces observed marginals (mean total variation distance
%%TVDMEAN%%), and projected acceptance differs by 75~percentage points between
contrasting profiles (55--83 across transfer factors). Cross-national ISSP data
place the Finland-to-Spain transfer factor at %%TA0%% and widen the population
projection to %%TPOP%%. Anticipatory governance theory supplies the design
requirements, and the results state when LLM-assisted projections can inform
pilots and measurement and when they cannot.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} large language models; human--AI division of labour;
