\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
%=============================================================================

Results are reported in the order of the validation design
(Section~\ref{sec:step5}): what the generator reproduces, how it compares with
an independent survey, and then, in Section~\ref{sec:scenarios}, the projection
for the outcome the national survey does not measure. All figures can be
recomputed with the scripts of Appendix~\ref{app:code}.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Validation of the language-model extraction}
\label{sec:res_extraction}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The language model extracted %%AUDENTRIES%% entries from the 11 studies, of
which %%AUDPROPOSED%% were proposed for the five target outcomes. The human
review accepted %%AUDACCEPTED%% of these as proposed, corrected
%%AUDCORRECTED%%, and excluded %%AUDEXCLUDED%% (Table~\ref{tab:audit}). All
recorded corrections concern the mapping of an item to a construct, a polarity
or a scale; none concerns the transcription of a reported number. The most
frequent error was construct mismatch: items on the environmental benefits of
recycled plastic were assigned to perceived safety, and an item on second-hand
products to acceptance of recycled material. The second was polarity: four risk
statements were entered as safety statements without reversal, which would
have turned disagreement with ``recycled plastics may be contaminated'' into
low perceived safety. Double counting, where a construct score and its items
from the same respondents were entered as separate evidence, would have
inflated the pooled sample size.

The same kinds of error occurred among the entries mapped to variables the CIS
already measures, where weekly meat consumption was treated as reduced plastic
consumption and interpersonal trust as institutional trust
(Section~\ref{sec:step3}). Their cost can be measured: used as priors,
those entries recovered masked CIS variables with a mean total variation
distance of $0.110$, against $0.038$ with a flat prior. In this audit the
errors concentrated in meaning rather than transcription, so the accuracy of
the extraction step has to be assessed at the level of construct mapping. Query parsing
by the interface has not been evaluated (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

%%CTGANVMIN%%--%%CTGANVMAX%%\% of its CIS value; TVAE inflates it by a factor of
%%TVAEVMIN%% to %%TVAEVMAX%%, so it keeps every dependency but not its magnitude.

Table~\ref{tab:vratios} reports dependency preservation pair by pair.
Dependencies within thematic blocks are kept (environmental concern with
recycling, %%VREC%%), whereas those that cross blocks, in particular between
demographics and attitudes, are attenuated to between a fifth and two fifths of
their CIS strength; %%KEPT%% of the ten remain statistically detectable. This
follows from block-wise structure learning: variables in different blocks are
linked only through the cross-block screening of Step~1. Joint distributions
across blocks are therefore less faithful than marginals. For the target
outcome this matters less, because the transfer model is evaluated on each
synthetic individual's own characteristics; it matters for any analysis that
relies on associations among observed attitudes. %%SEEDTEXT%%

\subsection{External comparison with an independent Spanish survey}
\label{sec:res_ipsos}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

One item of the Ipsos survey for Spain \citep{ipsos2022} is loosely comparable
with a CIS variable: support for an international treaty on plastics against
environmental concern (V15, top three of five categories). The synthetic share
is %%IPSOSBVS%% against 90\% in Ipsos, a difference of %%IPSOSERR%%; the CIS
itself gives %%IPSOSCIS%%. The difference therefore reflects the two
instruments, not the generator. The other Ipsos items have no counterpart in
the CIS: an earlier comparison of manufacturer responsibility with V27 matched
an item on paying higher taxes, coded from ``strongly in favour'' to ``strongly
against'', and is discarded. This comparison is a plausibility check under
loose construct matching and does not bear on food-packaging acceptance.
